How it works: three steps

You don't need to know what a compressor is, which knob is which, or how your model's menus work.

1

🔌 Plug in

PatchPilot recognises the pedal and connects as soon as you plug in the cable. It reads all your patches and saves a dated backup before touching anything. No ports to choose, no boxes to tick. 0.9.1

2

💬 Ask for a sound

Press Ask for a sound and write it your way: warm blues crunch 80s clean with chorus. The AI builds it with your pedal's real effects and PatchPilot puts it on the pedal — effects, on/off, knobs. Or load 20–30 seconds of a song. 0.9.1

3

🎯 Fine-tune by talking

too muddy like before, less distorted you do it. The sound coach changes only what is needed and explains it in one line. Every step is a variant: one click takes you back. 0.9.1

Then you play: setlist, stage mode and songs that follow the tempo tell you which sound comes next.

🛡️ Your saved sounds are never overwritten

🎚️ Patch in use only
PatchPilot changes the sound like you do when you turn the pedal's knobs. It never saves over your patches: like it? save it; don't? change patch and it's back.
💾 Backup first
On pedals that allow it, all your patches are saved to a file with date and time before the first change — never overwritten.
⛔ Blocked forever
Factory reset and deleting files in the pedal are blocked in the code. Saving to memory and sending whole patches stay off until a model is tested.

What to say to the coach: examples

Write the way you'd talk to a guitarist friend: no knob or effect names needed.

To start, “Ask for a sound”: 70s crunch like AC/DC bright clean for arpeggios warm lead with a long delay tight metal distortion, no hiss ambient sound with a huge reverb SRV-style blues

To fine-tune: too muddy more aggressive less distorted more bass cut through the mix more like before but cleaner you do it

On the effects: add a light chorus remove the delay shorter reverb 80s tempo delay switch on a compressor

If you don't like it: go back start over explain what you changed

The coach uses only your pedal's effects and changes 1–3 things at a time. Every message is one request.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to know how my pedal works?

No. You plug it in and describe the sound in your own words: PatchPilot chooses the effects and sets the knobs on the pedal, then tells you in one line what it did. If you want to learn, “Explain this patch” explains every effect and knob.

Can PatchPilot ruin my patches?

PatchPilot works on the patch in use, like turning the pedal's own knobs, and never saves over your patches. Before the first change it saves a dated backup of all patches (on pedals that allow it). Factory reset and file deletion are blocked forever.

Which pedals does it support?

30 Zoom pedals — MS-50G, MS-60B, MS-70CDR, the MultiStomp+ series, G1on/B1on, G3/B3, G1/B1 FOUR, G2/B2 FOUR, G3n, G5n, G6, G11, B6 — plus Boss Katana MkI/MkII, GT-100, GT-1000, Valeton GP-200, NUX Mighty Lite BT MkII, Line 6 HX Stomp and any MIDI pedal for patch changes. Tested on a real MS-50G; the others are in test.

Can it copy the sound of a song?

With 0.9.1: load 20–30 seconds of a song or play it to the microphone. PatchPilot measures distortion, tone, delay, reverb and modulation on your computer and the AI builds a close patch with your pedal's effects. It works best with the guitar alone. The audio never leaves your computer.

Is there an Android app for Zoom MultiStomp pedals?

Zoom's Guitar Lab apps for these pedals are mostly for iPhone. PatchPilot's Android app is coming soon on Google Play, with automatic pedal recognition, the AI sound coach and tone from a song; today PatchPilot runs on Windows, macOS and Linux.

Is PatchPilot an alternative to Guitar Lab?

Yes, and it also runs on Linux and works with older models such as the MS-50G, G1on and G3 that have no app. It doesn't install Zoom's effect files: for those you still need Zoom's software.

What does the AI send over the internet?

Only your request and the patch values (numbers). Never audio: songs and microphone are measured on your computer. Everything else works offline.
