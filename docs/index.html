PATCHPILOT

One app for 16 Zoom pedals — on Windows, Mac and Linux

PatchPilot connects your Zoom MultiStomp+, G1 FOUR, B1 FOUR, G2 FOUR, B2 FOUR, G6, G11 or B6 to a Windows, Mac or Linux computer (Android app coming soon on Google Play) with a USB cable and shows what is inside: patches, effects, parameters and the tuner. Zoom has a separate app for each pedal, almost all of them for iPhone only: PatchPilot puts them in one app.

What you can do

Look inside your pedal. The full patch list with names, each effect slot on or off with its parameters, and a tuner on the screen. Import your own .ZD2 effect files to see effect and parameter names.

Back up your sounds. Save every patch of your pedal to a file. Get a readable sound sheet for each patch — effects, on/off, parameters — to print, save or share.

Find and compare. “Where did I use that delay?” Search your patches by effect, and compare two patches to see only what is different.

Setlist and stage mode. Put your songs in order and choose the patch for each one — one patch per pedal if you use more than one. On stage, a dark full-screen view shows in big letters which patch to select and what comes next.

✨ New in 0.9

Features you will not find in other pedal editors, a library shared by everyone, and the Mac version. They are in the download now; like the rest of the beta, they still wait for tests on real pedals.

🔊 All your patches at the same volume. No more jumps when you switch patch live. Play the same riff with each patch for 6 seconds: PatchPilot measures how loud it sounds to the ear (LUFS, pauses ignored) and tells you the patch level that makes every patch even. Measure again to check.
Windows, macOS and Linux, with the pedal's output going into the computer (USB audio pedal or audio interface). The sound is only measured, never recorded; nothing is written to the pedal.

🥁 Songs that follow the tempo. Give each song its tempo and split it into sections — intro, verse, chorus, solo — each with its bars and its patch. On stage press T: a bar of count-in, the beats on screen, the next section on its own, and in the last bar “GET READY: 1-03 Crunch” before every patch change. Works with Bluetooth page-turner pedals, and shows the delay times for the song's tempo.
Android, Windows, macOS and Linux. With a generic MIDI pedal the computer sends the patch of each section by itself.

🌊 Gradual transitions. Instead of jumping from one sound to the next, the sound changes little by little, like a virtual expression pedal: in the seconds you choose, or over a few bars for each section of the setlist. On Zoom pedals, preview the values halfway between two patches.
Windows, macOS and Linux, sent to a generic MIDI pedal (expression CC). On Zoom pedals sending comes after the tests on real pedals.

📚 Community patch library. A public repository of patches made by PatchPilot users, free for everyone (CC0), searchable by song, artist, genre and pedal. One click in PatchPilot shares your patch; an automatic check verifies it and, once approved, it appears in the library.
Only the patch is shared, never Zoom's effect files.

🎚️ A screen shaped like your pedal. The main screen draws your model, with the song bar: mouse wheel, arrow keys or a USB page-turner pedal change song, and you can search a song by name. Plus a needle tuner, a fader view in the editor, MIDI faders and an audio level meter.

🍎 PatchPilot for Mac. The same program as a .dmg for Apple Silicon (M1–M4) and Intel Macs, Java included. First launch: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.

Your pedal is safe

PatchPilot only reads. It never writes, saves or deletes anything in your pedal's memory: those commands are blocked in the code. Backups, sound sheets and setlists are files on your phone or computer. Changing patch from the app and editing parameters will come only after tests on real pedals.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an Android app for the Zoom MS-50G+, MS-60B+ or MS-70CDR+?
Zoom's Handy Guitar Lab apps for the MultiStomp+ pedals are for iPhone. PatchPilot is a program for Windows, macOS and Linux, with the Android app coming soon on Google Play, that reads patches, effects and parameters from these pedals over USB. It is a beta.

Can PatchPilot edit or save patches?
Not yet. The beta only reads from the pedal. Editing parameters and changing patch will be added step by step after tests on real pedals.

Which cable do I need?
A USB-C to USB-C cable, or an OTG adapter with your pedal's USB cable. MultiStomp+ pedals usually also need their power supply, because a phone may not power them.

Does it work on a computer?
Yes: Windows 10/11, macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel) and Linux, with the same functions. Java is included in the download. Close Guitar Lab first: the pedal's MIDI port works with one program at a time.

How much does it cost?
Free during the beta. The full version is €4.99, paid once per device, by card (Stripe) or PayPal; you get a personal unlock code.

Can PatchPilot make all my patches the same volume?
Yes, from version 0.9, on Windows, macOS and Linux: you play the same riff with each patch, PatchPilot measures the perceived loudness (LUFS) and tells you the patch level that makes them even. The sound is only measured, never recorded.

Is there a library of free Zoom patches?
Yes: the PatchPilot community library, a public repository of patches shared by users under CC0, searchable by song, artist, genre and pedal.

Is PatchPilot made by Zoom?
No. PatchPilot is an independent project, not affiliated with Zoom Corporation.
